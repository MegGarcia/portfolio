Simplifying navigation in Johnson & Johnson's cell-therapy tracking portal, where a confusing screen isn't just friction. It's a compliance risk.

01

The Problem

The portal verifies every hand-off of a personalized cell therapy. After 10 site visits in the US and Germany and 11 user interviews with planners, warehouse and QA staff, two issues stood out.

02

The Process

I kicked off with a full-day hybrid workshop, then led the work from sketches to high-fidelity designs. Along the way I directed two designers and met with engineering weekly to keep effort proportional to each problem.

03

Two Key Decisions

Removing the intermediary page

Every workflow sat behind an extra page just to pick a task, and a wrong pick meant starting over. Users still needed its patient details, so I moved them into a side panel on the landing page and removed the page.

Making "home" findable globally

Clicking the logo to go home is a North American habit, so EMEA users got stranded. Breadcrumbs and global search were costly on Salesforce, so I added a simple home icon: recognized everywhere, with nothing to translate.

04

What Users Said

"I finally understand how to move around the portal!"

A user from Europe after using the new navigation. She finally had navigation that aligned with her expectation of how to move through the portal.

05

The Outcome

Shipped across two releases in 2025. That year the portal reached the industry UX-Lite benchmark (78%) for the first time. Alongside the two decisions, I also shipped:

  • Workflow checklist
  • Order overview
  • Reprint without modify
  • Modification mode
  • Resources & reports
  • Consolidated shipment tracking

Midway, I also led the platform's rebrand to Johnson & Johnson's 2023 identity, from design to hand-coded production components.
